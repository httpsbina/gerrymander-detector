Texas Congressional Redistricting

The 2025 Texas congressional map is a statistical outlier. No retained state in the validated 30,000-state post-burn-in ensemble produced as few Harris-won districts as PLANC2333.

Why I started here

Texas is one of the newest states to redraw its district lines, and most gerrymandering projects haven't analyzed these maps yet. It's also an unusual case because Texas did a mid-decade redistricting in 2025, only four years after the last round. States normally wait until after the census every ten years.

With 38 congressional districts, it's one of the largest and most complex states to redistrict. I figured if the pipeline could handle Texas, it could handle anywhere.

The 2025 map is currently being challenged in LULAC v. Abbott. A three-judge federal panel heard nine days of testimony from 23 witnesses and found substantial evidence of racial gerrymandering. The Supreme Court later reversed the injunction on procedural grounds, but the full trial is still pending.

Results

The final analysis retains 30,000 post-burn-in ReCom states. Every generated plan contains 38 contiguous congressional districts and stays within +/-0.1% of ideal district population. The 2024 Harris-Trump presidential vote is used as the partisan proxy.

Assumptions

Every analysis makes simplifications. Here are mine and why they matter.

Partisan proxy: 2024 presidential race

I used the Harris vs. Trump results to measure how each precinct leans. This follows the approach in Duchin's expert testimony in the Pennsylvania case and the MGGG Lab's published work. A stronger version would test multiple statewide races to confirm the finding holds.

Two constraints: population and contiguity

The ensemble requires contiguous districts and population balance within +/-0.1%. It does not yet impose explicit compactness, county-preservation, or Voting Rights Act constraints.

Mismatched boundaries

The final pipeline uses Census-block population rather than precinct-centroid population assignment. Block records are aggregated to the validated 2024 precinct geography. The final precinct dataset contains 9,712 units, including 322 zero-population units, and preserves the exact statewide population total.

Sample size

The final analysis retains 30,000 post-burn-in states. Because successive ReCom states are autocorrelated, these states are not treated as 30,000 independent observations. Effective sample sizes are approximately 98 for seat count, 92 for efficiency gap, and 118 for mean-median.
