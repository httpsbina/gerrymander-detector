How I build the comparison

I sample alternative congressional assignments under recorded population and contiguity constraints, then compare their metrics with enacted assignments.

Inputs and sampling

I obtain election and Census inputs from the Redistricting Data Hub, join them by block ID and construct the shared-boundary graph. The Texas graph has 9,712 units; I retain the geographic inputs within each experiment so that changing the starting assignment leaves those inputs fixed.

I run ReCom from recorded starting maps and seeds. A proposal selects adjacent districts, merges their units and uses a random spanning tree to seek a population-balanced split; valid proposals are accepted under the implemented graph-contiguity and population constraints.

I use GerryChain, following the ReCom method of DeFord, Duchin and Solomon (2021). I record Democratic-majority districts, efficiency gap, cut edges and mean-median for the sampled assignments.

Comparing runs

I compare the enacted scores with the sampled distribution and assess independent chains within starting-map groups and jointly. I check convergence before freezing the numerical results, keeping diagnostics with the run record.

Limitations

The comparison depends on the election model and implemented constraints. I have not established uniform sampling over valid plans, and population and graph contiguity cover only part of the legal requirements. I treat tail frequencies as descriptive; they provide neither calibrated p-values nor a direct finding of legislative intent.
