# Geographic comparison update

I compared the same saved assignments with different geographic weightings; no votes or party labels enter these calculations.

Texas has more raw cut adjacencies than every saved checkpoint, though 436 of those cuts lie inside simulation units where the stored maps cannot cut. Its between-unit component is 2,965; 45 of 5,560 checkpoints have at least that many. Its total internal boundary is shorter than every saved checkpoint. Both NC maps also have shorter boundaries than every checkpoint, with cut counts above their simulated medians. Density-weighted boundary length remains low for all three enacted maps.

Area weighting changes the dispersion assessment most in Texas: its uniform-area radius falls within the simulated distribution, while its population radius exceeds every saved checkpoint. The median district perimeter compactness remains higher than every saved checkpoint in both states, so that finding survives replacing the mean with the median.

The county table identifies where the population-distance gap occurs; county_contributions.png shows the largest positive contributions. The chain half-history table shows where the unresolved Texas dispersion drift occurs.

## Limitations

These comparisons remain conditional on the ensemble, with unresolved convergence for some geographic metrics. They do not establish partisan intent or a calibrated probability of gerrymandering. The original numerical freezes remain unchanged.
